# Getting non-members onto top-ranked and private courses

Research report, 23 November 2026. Written for the owner.

**How this was produced.** Twelve researchers each took one channel (donated-round auctions, association play days, competitions, paid networks, media and architecture societies, resorts and hotels, clubs' own policies, outing platforms, official feeds, competitors and demand, plus the legal question). A second agent re-opened each channel's ten highest-value sources and tried to refute them. A critic then named three missing channels, which were researched but not re-checked. Three strategists and a final writer worked only from that material. The full source list is in `access-source-catalog.md`. Nothing here is legal advice.

**One correction made after the report was written.** Member For a Day's terms were re-read on 24 November. They allow links to its home page, auction pages and scheduling pages, from your own site, with no implied endorsement, and it can withdraw that at any time. The "deep linking" they bar is displaying its pages inside another site. Its ban on extracting or indexing "by any means" and on commercial use of its content stands, so listing its lots with facts still needs its written yes.

## The short answer

**Yes, build the catalog of links.** Publishing a link with a few facts in our own words (club, close date, price) is sound under copyright law. "We're not really scraping then" needs two corrections. A program that visits a no-bots platform to learn the link and the date is still scraping, even if a link is all it keeps. And a few sites restrict the link itself. So each fact must arrive by a lawful route: the organizer's own page, written permission, an allowed feed or sitemap, search results, an organizer's submission, or a person reading the page.

**Yes, drive the timeline from the auction close**, but each entry needs three dates: opens, closes and play-by, each marked stated, estimated or expected.

**Access to the highest-ranked private clubs cannot be made a commodity.** Supply there is a few auctioned foursomes and one or two outings a year. What can be made common is knowing every sanctioned chance and when it opens and closes. No site does that today.

**Where the supply is.** Top-ranked clubs appear mainly in about 20 golf-organization auctions that repeat in the same week each year (Rounds 4 Research opened with more than 1,900 rounds in April 2026), on Member For a Day, and at two media societies. Volume sits at regional private clubs, through charity outings and association days.

**The cost.** The best sources need a written yes or a person a few times a year. That is your main decision, because the site is meant to run unattended.

## How access to a private club actually works (first principles)

A private club's tee sheet belongs to its members, through a board or an owner. Clubs do not sell rounds to strangers. They lend them, a day or a foursome at a time, to someone they trust, and that intermediary sells the spots and publishes the page. Five holders release access:

1. **The club lends a closed day**, usually a Monday, to a charity, golf association, media society or tournament.
2. **Staff donate a foursome** to their trade body's cause: superintendents to Rounds 4 Research, PGA professionals to PGA REACH auctions, clubs to state association foundations.
3. **Members donate a round with themselves as host**, through junior-golf auctions or Member For a Day.
4. **Owners whose business is selling golf**: resorts, governments, private clubs with lodging.
5. **The club itself, rarely**: young clubs building a membership, and Augusta-area clubs in Masters week.

Clubs agree for revenue on an idle day, a member's cause or standing with their association. Supply is capped by goodwill and the tee sheet. Member-owned clubs also face a confirmed IRS limit: no more than 15% of gross receipts from non-member use of facilities. Verifiers flagged the research's "one day per club per year" as overstated.

Three consequences follow:

- **Listings are scattered**, because the intermediary publishes, not the club: 1,309 items on 506 sites in the earlier pass.
- **Nobody publishes an inventory feed.** The verifier found no auction platform with a public listings feed or working affiliate program. eBay's API is the exception; it is contract-gated and showed no private-club supply.
- **Supply repeats.** Youth on Course has used the same November slot three years running; 7 to 10 November 2027 is already posted.

## The best sources and how to get each one lawfully

1. **GCSAA Rounds 4 Research.** More than 1,900 rounds at opening and $936,298 raised in 2026. One event link now; lot-by-lot listings need GCSAA's written consent. Do not crawl the bidding host.
2. **The recurring auction calendar**: about 20 organizers, including Youth on Course, AJGA Foundation, PGA REACH sections, Carolinas PGA and state association foundations. Hand-kept links to each organizer's own announcement page, never the bidding platform. WinningBidder bars unauthorized links; AJGA allows a home-page link only.
3. **Charity outings.** Play Private Golf's allowed sitemap, plus search results and organizer submissions for the long tail. Add what it lacks: it shows no deadline, and a price on 11% of events.
4. **The Golfer's Journal.** 46 events in 2026, about 28 at US private clubs. Bots banned; links expressly allowed, removed on request. Hand entry plus a permission email. Its 2027 list today is 12 waitlisted trips and no US private-club days.
5. **Fried Egg Golf.** 35 listings for 2027, with on-sale dates from 6 January to 29 April already published. Bots banned; terms silent on links. Hand entry plus a permission email.
6. **Member For a Day.** A 2024 press release claims auctions at 97 of the top 100 US courses. Written permission first; until then, link the charity's own announcement.
7. **Association schedule pages.** Crawl only association-owned pages rated allowed and hand-enter the rest. Get consent before any link to Maryland, New York State or Wisconsin. Never fetch Golf Genius or BlueGolf.
8. **Operator access pages.** One hand-written record per course, with a last-checked date.
9. **Golf Moose and Greenskeeper.org.** Permission request.
10. **Open-entry competitions.** USGA qualifiers through each association's own page; the Golf Digest Open as a link.
11. **The Golf Wire RSS feed.** Rated allowed; it carried Youth on Course's dates ten days ahead.
12. **32auctions sitemap.** Provisional: the research disagrees on its terms and yield.

**Where the three strategies disagreed, and what I chose:**

- **Member For a Day's rank.** One strategy put it second; two put it sixth or seventh. I chose sixth: nothing can be listed until it replies, and its terms bar indexing "by any means" and commercial use of its content. It still gets one of the first permission emails.
- **Timeline or club pages first.** Timeline first, with a club page only where a door is known. Demand is unmeasured, and hundreds of empty club pages could hurt search standing.
- **Alerts.** Calendar and RSS feeds without accounts, not email, for now. Email adds a dependency, privacy duties and cost.
- **Hand entry before a reply.** Yes for The Golfer's Journal and Fried Egg, because the first on-sale is 6 January. One strategy leaves Fried Egg to you, so it is on the decision list. Golf Moose waits for permission.

## Why a link catalog is allowed and where the line is

This is a plain-language read of the research, not legal advice.

- **Publishing.** Facts cannot be copyrighted (Feist). In Ticketmaster v. Tickets.com, event date, place and price were free facts and deep links were not infringement. Wording and pictures are not free: a service that sent headlines, links and short excerpts lost (AP v. Meltwater). So: our own summaries, plain text links, no images, logos or framing.
- **The checker's correction.** That holds for copyright only. The live risk is contract. This project has read and logged each platform's terms, so assume they bind. Where terms restrict links or commercial use, the link itself can be a breach; such a claim survived summary judgment in Ticketmaster's 2003 ruling.
- **Collecting.** A crawler on a no-bots site is scraping whatever it keeps. A person reading a public page and typing the facts is a reasonable reading of a robot clause, not settled law. Scripts and AI browsing agents do not count as a person.
- **Search results.** No authority says finding a link through search puts the site outside a platform's terms, and a 2026 suit against a search-scraping vendor and its customer was allowed to proceed. Use search to find links only.
- **Sites that restrict links.** TPC (text link to its home page only), Invited and Troon (written authorization), AJGA and Wisconsin (home page only), BlueGolf, Maryland, New York State, WinningBidder.
- **Closed gates.** A 403 or a Cloudflare challenge is not worked around. Any objection is final.

Every source record needs two rules: how the site may learn the facts, and how it may link.

## The timeline: what date ends each kind of opportunity and when each channel appears in the year

| Opportunity | Date that ends it | Also store |
|---|---|---|
| Auction lot | The organizer's stated close, not the platform header | Play-by date; flags for extended bidding and "ends in the room" |
| Charity outing | Registration deadline or sell-out; otherwise play date minus 7 days, labeled estimated | Early-bird date |
| Association day | Entry close, about 6 to 12 days before play | Registration-open time, the real deadline for lottery and sell-out days |
| Media-society day | Request-window close (The Golfer's Journal); sell-out (Fried Egg) | On-sale date and time |
| USGA qualifier | The national entry deadline | Fills-early flag |
| Resort or municipal course | None: a rolling window in days | Opens-on date |

Two traps: Minnesota's auction was announced to close 12 April; the platform showed 13 April. A New Jersey auction's header showed 30 September when bidding ended in late July, in the room.

Entries move from expected to announced, open, closing soon and closed. A closed edition becomes next year's expected row, which keeps the site useful in winter.

The year, on the 2026 pattern:

- **November to December.** Next-year calendars post (Fried Egg, the Carolinas schedule). The Prairie Club's Draw is 7 December.
- **January to March.** Chambers Bay advanced booking opens 1 January; Fried Egg on-sales begin 6 January; association registration opens from 1 February to mid March.
- **April.** The densest auction window: Masters week (NCGA Foundation, WA Golf Foundation, PGA REACH Philadelphia), then late April (Rounds 4 Research, New England PGA).
- **May to October.** Outing season, mostly Mondays, plus association days and qualifiers.
- **October to mid November.** Carolinas PGA, AJGA, Carolinas Golf Foundation, First Tee Massachusetts, Youth on Course.

As of 23 November 2026, every 2026 auction found has closed.

## What to do in the first 90 days

Day 1 is 24 November; day 90 is about 22 February.

1. **Week 1.** Answer the owner decisions. Reconcile the allowed-source list with the 14 October terms check, which recorded only Play Private Golf as allowed; take the stricter reading where research passes conflict (Virginia, the Carolinas, 32auctions).
2. **By early December.** Ship the data model as a migration, tests first: organizer, series, edition; three labeled dates; state; acquisition route; link rule; last-checked date.
3. **By 4 December.** You send the first permission emails: GCSAA, Member For a Day, The Golfer's Journal, Fried Egg. Then Golf Moose, New England PGA and the associations, starting with the Carolinas.
4. **December.** Hand-enter what has a date now, re-checking each on its own page: The Prairie Club Draw, Nevada Golf's 15 December day, Chambers Bay, Fried Egg's on-sale dates, Old Barnwell's April window. Seed about 20 auction organizers as expected 2027 rows.
5. **January.** Add the organizer submit-a-link form and The Golf Wire feed. Write access records for about 25 pay-to-play courses.
6. **January to February.** Weekly pass over association schedules as they post; enter The Golfer's Journal's spring request windows. Chase unanswered emails once, then apply the fallbacks.
7. **Day 90.** Count listings by channel, stated against estimated dates, replies received and what visitors used.

## What nobody else does

- **Every channel in one place.** Play Private Golf, the only free public directory, lists charity outings only, and no charity-auction search engine exists.
- **Deadlines and opening times**, with expected windows shown before an auction is announced.
- **The real first-year cost** and what you need first: membership, handicap, caddie fees.
- **A host tag** on every round: unaccompanied, professional-hosted or member-hosted.
- **Honest closed doors**, each with a source and a last-checked date.

A rival can copy links and facts. Permission letters, organizer relationships, date history and freshness are slower to copy.

## Risks and open questions

- **Thin at the top.** Copy that promises broad access to the highest-ranked clubs would be untrue.
- **Demand is unmeasured.** The evidence is forum threads and article comments.
- **Permissions may not come**, and all are revocable. Without GCSAA, 1,900 rounds stay one link.
- **Pushback.** An August 2026 NGCOA article tells course owners to send removal requests to third-party listers.
- **Positioning.** "Commoditizing access" would alarm the clubs and donors whose goodwill is the supply. Call it a calendar of every sanctioned way a non-member can play.

**Not checked:**

- The volunteering, university and raffle research was not independently verified.
- Some figures rest on search snippets, including Nebraska's auction dates and Michigan's Golf Days.
- The 2026 court decisions were confirmed from secondary sources only. The law of South Carolina, where Member For a Day's terms send disputes, was not checked.
- How the current search vendor obtains its results was not checked.
- Which host clubs are private often rests on the researcher's own knowledge.

**Open:** will GCSAA share its lot list, will Member For a Day say yes, and who does the hand entry?
