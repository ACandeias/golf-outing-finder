Golf Outing Finder is a free, nationwide directory of golf outings that anyone can pay to enter, at every kind of course: municipal, public, semi-private, private and resort. If you're running one, we'd like to list it. There's no fee, no account to create and nothing to sign. This guide explains how listings get here, what we show, what helps us get your details right, and how to ask for a correction or removal.

## Listing is free

We don't charge organizers to be listed, and we don't sell entries. The site is supported by display ads. Every listing has a Register button that sends golfers straight to your own registration page, so you keep the relationship with your players and handle payments however you already do.

## How outings get listed

Most listings are found automatically. Each night, our system looks for public pages that announce golf outings, in a few places:

- **Organizer websites.** The event page on your charity's, school's, club's or association's own site.
- **Registration platforms.** Public event pages on the golf and fundraising platforms many organizers use to take sign-ups.
- **Golf event directories and calendars.** Other public listings of outings, which often point back to the organizer's page.
- **Search.** Searches for outings by city and by course.

You can also send us your event directly. The suggest form takes a link to your event page plus an optional note. Anyone can use it, whether you're the organizer, a volunteer or a golfer who spotted an event we're missing.

## Our crawler

The pages are fetched by our crawler, GolfOutingFinderBot. It identifies itself by name, follows robots.txt, spaces out its requests and visits each page about once a week to keep listings current. It doesn't log in, submit forms, keep cookies or download images. If your robots.txt blocks it, we can't read your page, so we can't list your outing from it. Our [crawler page](/bot) has the details, including how to block it if you prefer.

## What we show on a listing

From your public page, we pull the facts a golfer needs to decide:

- the event name and organizer
- the date and the shotgun time
- the course, matched to our course records so the listing shows the course type
- the price per player and per foursome, or "Foursomes through sponsorship" if you only sell teams through sponsor packages
- what's included, such as breakfast, lunch, dinner, cart, range, a gift or contests
- the format, such as scramble or best ball
- whether registration is open, waitlisted, sold out or cancelled
- your registration link

Each listing has a short summary that we write in our own words, 300 characters at most. We don't copy your text, and we don't use your images or logos. Every listing links back to your page as the source, and shows a "Last verified" date so golfers know when we last checked it.

If your organization is a charity, the listing shows a "Charity" label when we can match it to IRS records. If we can't confirm it, the listing reads "Fundraiser, charity status unverified". Including your organization's legal name and tax ID on your page helps us match it.

## How to make your page easy to list

The clearer your page, the more accurate your listing. A few things help a lot.

### Put the key details in plain text

Write the date, course name, city, shotgun time, prices and what's included as regular text on the page. Details that only appear inside an image or a flyer are hard for any website, including search engines, to read. If you post a flyer, put the same details in text nearby.

### Use the course's full name

"Maple Hills Golf Club, Springfield" is much easier to match than "the Hills". We match each outing to a specific course, and when the name or city is unclear, we hold the listing rather than guess.

### List prices clearly

If you offer a single-player price and a foursome price, show both. If prices change, such as an early-bird rate, say when. When we can't find a price, the listing shows "See site".

### Link to registration

Include a clear link to register, on your own site or on the registration platform you use. That's where our Register button points.

### Keep the status current

When you sell out, open a waitlist or cancel, update your page. We recheck pages regularly, more often as the date gets close, and the listing follows your page.

### Add schema.org Event markup

If your site supports it, schema.org Event structured data helps search engines and directories read your event's name, date and location reliably. Many website builders and event plugins add it for you.

## Recurring events

If you run your outing every year, we keep last year's page and add an expected listing for next year at the same course, marked "Expected" with the month. When you announce the new date, the listing updates with the real details.

## Corrections and removals

If anything on a listing is wrong, or you'd rather your outing not be listed, email the address on our [corrections page](/corrections). Include the link to the listing, what should change or that you'd like it removed, and a way for us to confirm you're with the organizer, such as an email from your organization's domain. Removals take effect on the next nightly update. Corrections to your own page also flow through automatically when we next check it.

## Add the badge

If your outing is listed, you're welcome to add a small "Listed on Golf Outing Finder" badge to your event page. It's a plain image and link with no script. The HTML is on our [badge page](/listed).

## Next step

Make sure your event page has the date, course, prices and registration link in plain text, then [send us the link](/suggest). It's free, and the listing will link golfers straight back to you.
