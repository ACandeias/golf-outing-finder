Private clubs are usually open only to members and their guests. If you don't know a member, an outing is often the one way to play the course. Many private clubs host charity and community outings on their closed days, and most of these are open to anyone who pays the entry fee. This guide explains why clubs host outings, how to find them and what to expect when you go.

## Why private clubs host outings

Private clubs run on member dues, but most also host a handful of outside events each season. There are a few reasons:

- **Supporting the community.** Many clubs give a few dates a year to local charities, hospitals or schools, often because a member is involved with the cause.
- **Revenue.** Outings bring in money from green fees, carts, food and beverage, which helps the club's budget.
- **Using a quiet day.** Many private clubs close to members one day a week, often Monday, for maintenance. Outings often fill those days.

The number of outside outings varies widely. Some clubs host one or two a year; others host a dozen or more. Some clubs host none at all.

## Who can play

At most charity outings held at private clubs, anyone can register. You don't need a member's sponsorship or an invitation. You pay the entry fee on the organizer's page, show up and play.

A few kinds of private club events aren't open to the public:

- **Member-guest tournaments.** Members invite a guest. You can't sign up on your own.
- **Club championships and member events.**
- **Some corporate outings.** A company may rent the club for its clients and employees only.

If an event is limited to invitees, the organizer's page usually says so. Golf Outing Finder lists outings anyone can pay to enter.

## How to find them

**Search by course type.** On Golf Outing Finder, every listing shows the course type: municipal and public, semi-private, private or resort. Use the course type filter to show private clubs only, then narrow by date range and distance.

**Check course pages.** Each course with listed outings has its own page that shows past and upcoming outings. Many charity outings return to the same club every year, often on a similar date, so a past listing is a good hint about next season.

**Ask the club.** Some clubs share a list of the outside outings they're hosting, or will tell you which charities usually book dates. A call to the golf shop is a fair way to ask.

**Look early.** Popular outings at well-known private clubs can fill months ahead. Spring and early summer dates are often announced in winter.

The [map](/map) is another way to look, especially if you're willing to drive a bit for a particular course.

## What they cost

Outings at private clubs are usually priced higher than outings at public courses. A typical range might be $250 to $600 per player, with some well-known clubs higher. Foursome prices are often listed too. Prices vary a lot by region, the club and what's included, and these figures are only illustrative.

Part of the higher price reflects what the club charges for a non-member round, food and service. At a charity outing, part also goes to the cause. For more on how prices are set, see [how outing pricing works](/guides/how-outing-pricing-works).

## What's often included

Private club outings often include more service than you'd get at a public course. Depending on the club and the organizer, that can include:

- bag drop and club cleaning
- locker room access
- range balls and a warm-up area
- forecaddies or caddies
- a plated dinner or full buffet
- a nicer gift, like a golf shirt with the club's logo
- tips and service charges built into the fee

Each listing on Golf Outing Finder shows what's included. The organizer's page has the full details.

## What to expect on the day

**Check-in.** Arrive 60 to 90 minutes before the shotgun. Pull up to the bag drop, where staff will take your clubs to your cart. Ask where to park.

**Dress code.** Private clubs usually have stricter dress codes than public courses: collared shirts, tailored shorts or slacks, and soft-spike golf shoes. Many don't allow denim anywhere on the property. Read [what to wear to an outing at a private club](/guides/what-to-wear-private-club-outing) before you go.

**Phones.** Many clubs restrict phone calls in the clubhouse and on the course. Step outside or into the parking lot for calls.

**Tipping.** Some clubs don't allow tipping staff, while others welcome it. If you're not sure, ask at check-in. It's often built into the fee.

**Course conditions.** Greens at private clubs are often faster than what you're used to. Spend a few minutes on the practice green.

**The format.** Most private club outings use a scramble, which means your own score matters less and the pace stays steady.

## Getting a spot

- **Register early.** Some outings at private clubs sell out quickly.
- **Ask about singles.** Many accept single players and pair them with others. Use the "singles welcome" filter to find outings that list a single price.
- **Look for a waitlist.** If an outing is sold out, the organizer may keep a waitlist.
- **Sponsor.** Some outings include a foursome with a hole or cart sponsorship.

## Treat it the same as any outing

An outing at a private club is a good day of golf for a good cause. So is a $125 scramble at your local muni. The basics are the same: show up early, keep pace, follow the dress code and say thanks to the volunteers. You don't need to be a low handicap to play at a private club outing, and you don't need to know anyone there.
