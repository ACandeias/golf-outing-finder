"Golf outing" covers a lot of ground. A hospital foundation's annual classic, a chamber of commerce scramble and a company's day out with its clients can all look alike on the course: shotgun start, carts, a scramble, lunch and prizes. Underneath, they're often run for different reasons, by different kinds of organizers, for different crowds.

This guide compares corporate and charity outings, explains the business-focused events in between, and covers what that means for you if you're deciding which to play or which to run.

## The short version

- A **charity outing** raises money for a nonprofit or a cause. Anyone who pays can usually play, and the day is built around fundraising.
- A **corporate outing** is hosted by a company, usually for clients, partners or employees. Many are invitation-only.
- In between are **business-focused outings that are open to the public**, such as those run by chambers of commerce, trade associations and business groups, where networking is the main draw.

Golf Outing Finder lists outings that anyone can pay to enter. That includes charity outings and open business outings, but not private company events that only invited guests can attend.

## Charity outings

### Purpose

The goal is to raise money. Entry fees usually cover the course's costs, and the money raised comes from sponsorships, extras like mulligans and raffles, auctions and donations. Our guide to [how charity golf outings work](/guides/how-charity-golf-outings-work) goes into the details.

### Who plays

Supporters of the cause, families and friends of the organizers, local businesses that sponsor, and golfers who simply like playing outings. You'll see every skill level, and plenty of people who only play a few times a year.

### Feel of the day

Usually warm and community-minded. Expect a welcome that explains the cause, sponsor signs on every tee, a raffle table and an awards program that often includes a short talk about what the money will do.

### Pricing

Prices range widely, from modest entry fees at public courses to higher prices at private clubs and resorts. The price often includes a charity component on top of the course's costs.

### Taxes

Part of a charity outing's entry fee may be deductible as a charitable gift, but only the portion above the value of what you receive, such as golf, a cart and a meal. Our guide to whether [charity golf entry fees are tax deductible](/guides/are-charity-golf-entry-fees-tax-deductible) explains the general rules. It's not tax advice.

## Corporate outings

### Purpose

A company hosts the day to build relationships. That might mean thanking clients, entertaining prospects, rewarding a sales team or bringing a department together. Golf gives people four or five hours together in a relaxed setting, which is hard to get any other way.

### Who plays

Usually invited guests: clients, partners, vendors and employees. Many corporate outings never appear publicly at all, because the company is paying and choosing who comes.

### Feel of the day

Often polished and focused on hospitality, with branded gifts, a nicer meal and plenty of time to talk. Some companies pair their outing with a charity, donating a portion of the day's costs or inviting a nonprofit to speak.

### Costs

Guests usually don't pay. The company covers course fees, food, gifts and prizes. For the company, the cost is generally a business expense rather than a charitable gift, though the tax treatment depends on the details.

## Business outings open to the public

This middle category is where many golfers first meet "corporate" golf. Chambers of commerce, trade associations, professional groups and business networks often run outings that anyone can register for. They're about meeting people in a local business community or an industry, and they usually raise money for the group's programs or a scholarship fund.

On Golf Outing Finder, outings run by trade associations and similar business groups carry a "Trade group outing" label so you can tell them apart. They often sell foursomes to businesses that want to bring clients, as well as single spots for individuals who want to meet people.

## Which should you play?

It depends on what you want from the day.

- **You want to support a cause.** A charity outing is the natural choice. Look for the "Charity" label if confirmed charity status matters to you.
- **You want to meet people in your industry or town.** A trade group or chamber outing is built for that.
- **You want a good day of golf at a reasonable price.** Either kind can work. Compare what's included and the course type, and read the organizer's page.
- **You're going alone.** Filter by singles welcome. Both kinds often accept individuals and place them on teams. Our guide to [playing an outing as a single](/guides/playing-an-outing-as-a-single) explains how.

## If you're the one organizing

If your company wants to host an outing, you have two broad options. You can run a private corporate event for invited guests, or partner with a charity and run an open outing that also serves as client entertainment. The second option brings in more players and sponsors and supports a cause, but it means sharing control of the day with the nonprofit. Many businesses also join existing charity outings as sponsors instead of running their own.
