Most golf outing listings give a start time like "11:00 a.m. shotgun" or "8:00 a.m. shotgun start." That means every group in the field tees off at the same moment, each from a different hole. It's the standard way to run an outing, and it shapes the whole day: when you arrive, where you start and when you finish. Here's how it works.

## The basic idea

On a normal day at a golf course, groups tee off one after another from the first hole, usually 8 to 12 minutes apart. With 36 groups, the last group would start about six hours after the first.

A shotgun start puts every group on the course at once. Each group drives to an assigned hole, waits, and starts when the signal goes off. If your team starts on hole 7, you play 7 through 18 and then 1 through 6. Everyone finishes at roughly the same time, which is what makes the lunch-before and dinner-after schedule of an outing possible.

The name comes from the old practice of firing a shotgun to signal the start. Today it's more often an air horn, a siren, an announcement over a loudspeaker or simply the posted start time.

## How many players fit

An 18-hole course has 18 tee boxes. With one foursome per hole, that's 72 players. Most outings put two groups on many holes, usually the par 4s and par 5s, labeled "A" and "B." That lets the course fit 120 to 144 players, depending on how many holes get doubled.

If your cart sign says "9B," your group starts on hole 9, but you wait for the "9A" group to hit their tee shots and move ahead before you tee off. Par 3s usually have only one group, since two groups waiting on a short hole would back things up.

## Before the start

A shotgun day runs on a tight schedule, so arriving on time matters more than it does for a regular tee time. A typical sequence for a noon shotgun:

- **10:30 to 11:00 a.m.** Check in at the registration table. Get your cart number, starting hole and rules sheet.
- **11:00 to 11:40 a.m.** Lunch, the range and the putting green.
- **11:40 a.m.** Rules announcement and sponsor thanks. Organizers often call players to the carts at this point.
- **11:45 a.m.** Carts leave the staging area in a line and head out to their holes. Some courses send carts out in order so they don't jam the paths.
- **12:00 p.m.** Start.

Plan to arrive 60 to 90 minutes before the shotgun time. If you're late, your group may have already left for the hole, and catching up means a cart ride across the course while the round is underway.

## Finding your hole

Your cart will usually have a sign showing your names and starting hole. Most courses post a map or have the cart staff point you in the right direction. If you're starting on a far-off hole, you might drive for 10 minutes to get there. When you arrive, park the cart near the tee, out of the way of the group ahead if you're the "B" group, and wait for the signal.

## During the round

Once the round starts, play it like any other, with a few differences:

- **Pace matters more.** Every hole has a group on it, so a slow team holds up the group behind and the group behind them. In a scramble, pick up your ball as soon as the team has chosen its shot.
- **You may skip the halfway house at the usual spot.** If you started on hole 14, your turn at the clubhouse comes at a different point. Many outings set up drink and snack stations on the course instead.
- **Contests are on fixed holes.** Closest to the pin might be on hole 6, so you'll get there whenever your rotation reaches it.
- **Volunteers may be at certain holes.** They might run a raffle game, sell mulligans or take photos.

Most outings use a scramble, which keeps the field moving. If you're new to the format, read [what is a golf scramble](/guides/what-is-a-golf-scramble).

## How long it takes

A full shotgun round with 120 to 144 players usually takes four and a half to five and a half hours. Doubled holes slow things down, since each "B" group waits on the tee. A scramble helps keep it closer to the low end.

After the last hole, groups head back to the clubhouse to turn in scorecards. Because everyone finishes at about the same time, the dinner and awards can start soon after.

## Morning, afternoon and double shotguns

**Morning shotguns** often start at 7:30 or 8:00 a.m., with breakfast before and lunch after.

**Midday shotguns** start around 11:00 a.m. to 1:00 p.m., with lunch before and dinner after. These are the most common for charity outings.

**Double shotguns** run two waves on the same day, one in the morning and one in the afternoon. They're used for big outings that sell more spots than one wave can fit. If an outing lists two start times, you'll pick one when you register.

## Where shotgun starts are common

You'll see shotgun starts at every kind of course. A municipal course might close for a Monday outing and run a 9:00 a.m. shotgun. A private club might host a Monday shotgun on its usual closed day for members. Resorts and semi-private courses do the same. The format doesn't change with the course type or the price.

Some smaller outings use tee times instead. If a listing shows a first tee time rather than a shotgun time, expect groups to start in sequence from the first and tenth tees, which is called a split-tee start.

## Tips for a smooth day

- Write down your starting hole and cart number when you check in.
- Use the restroom before you head out. If you start on a far hole, the clubhouse may be a long way off.
- Bring water and sunscreen. Drink stations aren't always on the first few holes you play.
- Know where the scorecard goes when you finish. The rules sheet will usually say.
