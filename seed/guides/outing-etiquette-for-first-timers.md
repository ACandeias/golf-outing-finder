Your first golf outing can feel like a lot. There's a registration table, a crowd of people in matching hats, carts lined up with names on them and somebody on a microphone. Then a horn goes off and everyone drives away at once. It's a fun day, and almost everyone there wants you to enjoy it.

This guide covers the unwritten rules: when to show up, what happens at check-in, how to keep pace, how to treat the staff and volunteers, and what to do after the round. None of it is complicated, and nobody expects perfection.

## Before the day

### Read the event page

The organizer's page usually tells you the shotgun time, the format, what's included and any dress code. Check it a few days ahead so you know whether breakfast is served, whether there's a dinner afterward and whether you need to bring anything.

### Know your team

If you registered as a foursome, confirm who's driving with whom. If you signed up alone, the organizer will place you on a team, and you may not find out who's on it until you arrive. That's normal. Our guide to [playing an outing as a single](/guides/playing-an-outing-as-a-single) covers what to expect.

### Dress for the course

Public and municipal courses tend to be relaxed, but most still expect golf attire: a collared shirt, golf shorts or slacks, and golf shoes or clean sneakers. Private clubs and resorts are often stricter, with rules on denim, collars and how shorts are cut. If you're heading to a private club, our guide on [what to wear to a private club outing](/guides/what-to-wear-private-club-outing) has the details.

## Arriving and checking in

### Arrive early

Plan to get there 45 minutes to an hour before the shotgun start. That gives you time to drop your bag, check in, find your cart, eat something and hit a few putts. Arriving at the last minute means your team waits for you while the whole field is trying to head out together.

### Bag drop

At many courses, staff take your clubs at the curb and put them on a cart. At others, you carry them to the cart yourself. Follow the signs or ask whoever is standing nearby.

### Check in at the registration table

Give your name, pick up anything included with your entry, and buy mulligans or raffle tickets if you want them. Volunteers usually run this table and they're often moving fast, so have your name ready and keep it brief if there's a line.

### Listen to the announcements

Before the start, someone will usually explain the format, the contest holes, the rules for any big prize and how scores are turned in. Even if you've played a hundred rounds, listen. Every outing has its own rules.

## On the course

### Head to your starting hole

With a shotgun start, each team begins on a different hole, and your cart or scorecard will say which. When the signal sounds, drive to your hole and start from there. Our guide to [how shotgun starts work](/guides/how-shotgun-starts-work) explains the whole process.

### Keep up the pace

Pace matters more at an outing than in a regular round, because every group is on the course at the same time. If one group falls behind, everyone behind them waits. A few habits help:

- Be ready to hit when it's your turn, even if you're not the farthest away. Most outings play "ready golf".
- In a scramble, once the team has picked the best shot, move to it and keep things moving.
- Take a practice swing or two, not five.
- If the group behind is waiting on every shot, pick up and move on. In a scramble, your team's score is safe.

### Pick up when it's not your ball

In a scramble, only one ball counts on each shot. Once it's clear yours won't be used, pick it up. Nobody needs you to putt out a 40-footer when your teammate's ball is already in.

### Respect contest holes

Read the sign before you hit. Closest to the pin and longest drive holes often have rules about which tee shot counts, and the hole-in-one prize may have strict conditions. If a volunteer is stationed on the hole, say hello and thank them.

### Take care of the course

Fix your ball marks, rake bunkers, replace or fill divots and keep carts where the course tells you to. Outings depend on courses being willing to host them.

### Be sensible about drinks

Many outings include a beverage cart, and having a drink with your team is part of the fun. Keep it within limits so you're safe driving the cart and home afterward.

## Volunteers, sponsors and staff

Most outings run on volunteers. They're giving up their day, so be patient and say thank you.

Sponsors make the event possible. If a business has a table at a hole, stop and say hello, even for a minute. It helps the organizer keep them coming back.

Tipping customs vary. At some courses and clubs it's customary to tip the bag drop and cart staff a few dollars, while some private clubs don't allow tipping at all. If you're unsure, ask the organizer or the pro shop.

## After the round

### Turn in your scorecard

Follow the instructions from the announcements. Check that the score adds up and that it's signed if they ask for that.

### Stay for the awards

Leaving right after the last hole is the most common first-timer mistake. The meal, the raffle drawing and the awards are where the organizer thanks sponsors and, at charity events, often shares what the money will do. It's also where you'll win your raffle prize, if you're lucky.

### Say thanks

A quick word to the organizer on your way out goes a long way. If you enjoyed it, tell them you'll be back next year.
