Private clubs usually have a dress code, and it applies to outing guests just as it does to members. The good news is that it's not complicated. If you dress the way most golfers do for a nice round, you'll be fine. This guide covers the basics, the common rules that catch people off guard and what to bring for the dinner afterward.

Every club sets its own rules, so treat this as a general guide. If the organizer's page or the club's site lists a dress code, follow that.

## The short version

- A collared golf shirt, or a mock-neck or collarless golf top where the club allows it
- Tailored golf shorts, slacks or a golf skirt, skort or dress
- Golf shoes with soft spikes or spikeless soles
- No denim, cargo shorts, gym shorts or T-shirts
- Hat facing forward, and off indoors at many clubs
- A layer for the weather and something neat for dinner

## Shirts and tops

A collared golf shirt is the safest choice. Polo-style shirts in a performance fabric or cotton are standard. Many clubs now accept mock-neck golf shirts and collarless golf tops designed for the course, but some don't, so a collar removes any doubt.

For women, sleeveless tops with a collar are widely accepted. Some clubs allow sleeveless tops without a collar, and some don't. Racerback tops and tank tops are often not allowed.

Most clubs ask that shirts be tucked in, especially traditional clubs. If your shirt has a straight hem designed to be worn out, it may be fine, but tucking in is the safer choice.

## Shorts, pants and skirts

Tailored golf shorts or slacks are standard. Clubs often have rules on:

- **Length.** Shorts are usually expected to reach near the knee. Very short shorts may not be allowed.
- **Style.** Cargo shorts, athletic shorts and drawstring shorts are often not allowed.
- **Denim.** Most private clubs don't allow jeans anywhere on the property, including the parking lot and clubhouse. This is the rule that catches the most people.

Golf skirts, skorts and golf dresses are widely accepted. Some clubs have a length rule here too.

Jogger-style golf pants are increasingly common, but not every club accepts them. If you're unsure, wear classic slacks.

## Shoes

Most clubs require golf shoes with soft spikes or spikeless soles. Metal spikes are almost never allowed. Some clubs accept clean athletic sneakers; others don't.

Bring a second pair of shoes for after the round. Many clubs ask that golf shoes not be worn in the dining room, or require you to change in the locker room. Leather shoes or clean casual shoes are a safe choice.

## Hats and accessories

A golf cap or visor is fine on the course. Wear it facing forward. Many clubs ask that hats come off inside the clubhouse, especially in the dining room. Some ask guests to keep sunglasses off their heads indoors, too.

Belts are expected with slacks and with shorts that have belt loops at many traditional clubs.

## Weather layers

Outings run rain or shine unless the course closes, so dress for the forecast. Our guide on [what happens when an outing is rained out](/guides/rained-out-golf-outings) covers how organizers handle bad weather.

- **Cool mornings.** A quarter-zip pullover, golf vest or sweater over your shirt works well. Hooded sweatshirts are often not allowed on the course at private clubs.
- **Rain.** A golf rain jacket and rain pants are fine. Bring an umbrella and a spare glove.
- **Heat.** Lightweight performance fabrics, a wide-brim hat where allowed, sunscreen and water.

## What to wear to dinner

The dinner and awards after the round are usually casual. Many players stay in their golf clothes and change their shoes. At some clubs, you may want a fresh shirt or a light sport coat, but that's uncommon at outings.

A few things to check:

- **Does the club have a separate clubhouse dress code?** Some clubs require collared shirts and long pants in the main dining room, even if shorts are fine on the course.
- **Is there a cocktail reception?** Larger outings sometimes have a reception before dinner with a slightly dressier code.
- **Can you use the locker room?** Many clubs open locker rooms to outing guests, so you can shower and change. The listing or organizer's page may mention this.

## Common mistakes

- **Denim in the parking lot.** At many private clubs, the dress code starts when you get out of the car.
- **Golf shoes in the dining room.** Bring a second pair.
- **Hats indoors.** Take it off at the door.
- **Untucked shirts.** Tuck in to be safe.
- **Phones.** Many clubs restrict calls in the clubhouse and on the course. This isn't about clothing, but it's often listed in the same rules.

## Does a muni outing need the same care?

Not usually. Municipal and public courses tend to be more relaxed, and some allow T-shirts or even denim. Still, a collared shirt is a good default at any outing, since it fits any course and any sponsor photo. The same goes for semi-private courses and resorts, where dress codes fall somewhere in between. A $125 muni scramble and a private club outing are both worth showing up for in clean, comfortable golf clothes.

## What if you're not sure?

- **Check the organizer's page.** Some outings list the dress code.
- **Check the club's site.** Many clubs post their dress code for guests.
- **Call the golf shop.** Staff get this question all the time.
- **Go traditional.** A collared shirt, tailored shorts or slacks, soft-spike shoes and a belt will work almost anywhere.

## Packing list

- Two golf shirts, in case of heat or rain
- Golf shorts, slacks or skirt
- Golf shoes and a second pair for the clubhouse
- A belt
- Golf cap or visor
- Pullover or vest
- Rain jacket and umbrella
- Sunscreen, water bottle, extra glove and balls
