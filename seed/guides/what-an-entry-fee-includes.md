A golf outing entry fee is a bundle. When you pay $150 or $400 to play, you're paying for a round of golf plus a set of extras the organizer has arranged with the course, and, at a charity event, a contribution to the cause. Knowing what's typically in that bundle makes it easier to compare outings and to avoid surprises on the day.

## The core: golf and a cart

Almost every outing fee includes:

- **Green fee.** Your round of 18 holes. A few outings are nine holes, and the listing should say so.
- **Golf cart.** Usually shared between two players. Walking outings exist but are rare, since a shotgun start with a full field moves best when everyone rides.
- **Practice facilities.** Many outings include range balls and access to the putting green before the round. At some courses, range balls are extra.

If you're comparing an outing fee with a normal tee time at the same course, this is the part to compare. A weekend round with cart at a municipal course might cost $40 to $90 on its own. The outing fee is higher because of everything else it includes and, at a charity event, the donation built into it.

## Food and drinks

Food is the next biggest piece, and it varies more than anything else.

- **Before the round.** A morning outing might include coffee and a breakfast buffet or breakfast sandwiches. A midday outing often includes a boxed lunch, a grill line or a buffet.
- **On the course.** Many fees include a set number of drink tickets, water and soft drinks at a halfway station, or snacks at sponsor tents. Some outings include beer from the beverage cart, and others sell it.
- **After the round.** Dinner is often the centerpiece of a charity outing, with awards, a raffle and sometimes an auction. It might be a buffet, a plated meal or a cookout on the patio.

When a listing says "lunch and dinner," that's two meals. When it says "food included," check the organizer's page for details.

## Gifts and prizes

Many outings hand each player a gift at check-in. It might be a sleeve of balls, a towel, a hat, a polo shirt or a small bag of items from sponsors. Larger outings sometimes give a more valuable item, like a golf shirt or a gift card.

Prizes for the winning teams and contest winners are usually covered by the organizer's budget or donated by sponsors. You don't pay anything extra to be eligible.

## Contests

Most outings run a few free contests during the round. Common ones include:

- **Closest to the pin** on one or more par 3s.
- **Longest drive** on a selected hole, sometimes with separate men's and women's divisions.
- **Straightest drive**, where the ball closest to a line down the middle of the fairway wins.
- **Hole-in-one** on a par 3, often with a car or cash prize backed by an insurance policy.

These are generally included in the fee. Paid extras, covered below, are separate.

## What usually costs extra

Plan to bring some cash or a card for:

- **Mulligans.** A redo of a shot, often sold in packs at check-in. Prices and limits vary by outing.
- **Raffle tickets.** Usually sold before and during the dinner.
- **Auction items.** At a silent or live auction.
- **Games on the course.** "Beat the pro" holes, putting contests or a chance to hit from a special tee.
- **Drinks beyond what's included.**
- **Tips.** At many private clubs, the bag drop and caddie staff are covered by a service charge in the fee; at other courses, a few dollars for the cart staff who clean your clubs is appreciated. Ask at check-in if you're unsure.

Our guide to [mulligans, raffles and contests](/guides/mulligans-raffles-and-contests) explains these extras and how they affect your day.

## Per player and per foursome pricing

Many outings price both ways. A foursome price is often a little lower than four single entries, and sometimes it includes a hole sponsorship or extra raffle tickets. Some outings sell foursomes only through sponsorship packages, so the listing might say "Foursomes through sponsorship" instead of showing a price. If you're playing alone or with one friend, look for a single price. Our guide on [how outing pricing works](/guides/how-outing-pricing-works) goes deeper on why prices vary.

## How fees differ by course type

The same basic bundle shows up at every kind of course, but the details shift.

- **Municipal and public courses.** Fees are often the lowest. A weekday muni scramble might be $100 to $175 per player and include golf, a cart, lunch and a few contests. Some include a simple dinner or a cookout on the patio.
- **Semi-private courses.** Often in the middle, with a clubhouse dinner and better range access.
- **Private clubs.** Fees are often higher, $250 to $600 or more per player, and the bundle may include a plated dinner, bag drop service, locker room access, caddies or forecaddies, and a higher-end gift.
- **Resorts.** Similar to private clubs, sometimes with lodging packages for multi-day events.

These ranges are illustrative. Prices vary widely by region, course and season.

## The charitable part of the fee

At a charity outing, part of what you pay is a donation and part pays for the golf, food and gifts you receive. If you're hoping to deduct some of the fee, the value of what's included matters, because only the amount above the fair market value of those items may be deductible. Read [are charity golf entry fees tax deductible](/guides/are-charity-golf-entry-fees-tax-deductible) for general information.

## How to check what's included

Before you register:

1. **Read the listing.** Each outing on Golf Outing Finder shows what's included, the price per player and per foursome, the date and shotgun time, and the course type.
2. **Check the organizer's page.** The Register button goes to the organizer's own site, which usually has the full schedule and menu. That page has the final word.
3. **Look at the "Last verified" date.** We recheck listings over time, but details can change. If something important to you, like dinner or a cart, isn't clear, contact the organizer.
4. **Ask about dietary needs.** If you need a vegetarian or allergy-friendly meal, tell the organizer when you register.
