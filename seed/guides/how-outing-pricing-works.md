Look at a handful of golf outings in the same city and the prices can be all over the place. One scramble at a municipal course might cost a little over $100 a player, while an outing at a private club across town asks several times that. Both can be good value. The difference comes down to what you're paying for.

This guide explains how outing prices are usually set, what pushes them up or down, and how to compare listings on Golf Outing Finder. The figures here are illustrative. Prices vary by region, course and organizer.

## Per player and per foursome

Most outings sell entries in two ways.

- **Per player**, sometimes called a single entry. You sign up on your own, and the organizer places you on a team.
- **Per foursome**, a full team of four.

The foursome price is often four times the single price. Sometimes there's a small discount for buying a full team, and sometimes the foursome price is higher because it includes a sponsor sign or another perk. It's worth doing the math. If the foursome price divided by four is less than the single price, you're getting a deal for bringing your own group.

Some outings sell only foursomes, or only sell them as part of a sponsorship package. On Golf Outing Finder, those listings show "Foursomes through sponsorship" instead of a per-player price.

## What drives the price

### Course type

The course is usually the biggest single factor. Municipal and public courses tend to cost the organizer less per player, which often means lower entry fees. Semi-private courses sit in the middle. Private clubs and resorts usually charge organizers more, and many golfers see an outing as a rare chance to play a course they couldn't otherwise get on.

As a rough illustration, many public course scrambles fall somewhere around $75 to $200 per player, while private club outings often run higher, sometimes several hundred dollars. Treat those ranges as examples, not rules. Our guides to [outings at municipal courses](/guides/outings-at-municipal-courses) and [outings at private clubs](/guides/outings-at-private-clubs) cover each in more detail.

### Day of the week

Weekday outings, especially Mondays, are common because courses are less busy. Many private clubs close to members on Mondays and host outings then. Weekend dates at busy courses can cost organizers more, and that can show up in the price.

### What's included

An entry fee usually covers green fees and a cart, but the rest varies. Breakfast, lunch, dinner, drinks, range balls, a gift, contests and a caddie can all be included or left out. An outing with a sit-down dinner and a gift bag will cost more than one with a boxed lunch, even at the same course. Our guide to [what an entry fee includes](/guides/what-an-entry-fee-includes) goes through the usual items.

### The charity component

At charity outings, the entry fee often includes an amount on top of the course's costs that goes to the cause. That's part of why a charity outing can cost more than a regular round at the same course. Some of that amount may be tax deductible, but usually not the part that covers golf and food.

### Timing and early-bird pricing

Some organizers offer a lower early-bird price for people who register before a certain date, then raise it closer to the event. Others offer discounts for returning players, members of the organization or groups.

### Region

Costs vary across the country. The same kind of outing can cost noticeably more in some metro areas than in smaller towns, simply because course fees and food costs are higher.

## Extras you'll pay on the day

The entry fee is rarely the only cost. Plan for a few extras:

- mulligans and raffle tickets, often sold at check-in
- contests that charge a small entry fee
- drinks beyond what's included
- tips for bag drop or cart staff where that's customary

None of these are required. They're part of the fun, and at charity events they add to the money raised.

## Questions to ask before you pay

A price on its own doesn't tell you everything. Before you register, it's worth checking a few things on the organizer's page:

- **Is there a processing fee?** Some registration pages add a small fee at checkout.
- **What's the refund policy?** Many charity outings don't refund entries, especially for weather.
- **Can you swap players?** If someone in your foursome drops out, most organizers let you substitute, but it's good to know.
- **Is a sponsorship a better fit?** If you're a business, a package with a foursome and a sign may cost little more than the team alone.

## When a listing says "See site"

When an organizer's page doesn't state a price, or we can't read one clearly, the listing shows "See site". That doesn't mean the outing is expensive or that registration is closed. It just means the price is on the organizer's page, sometimes behind the registration button. Click through to find it.
