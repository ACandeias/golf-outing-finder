Municipal courses, the ones owned by a city, county or park district, host a large share of the golf outings in this country. Youth leagues, school boosters, fire and police associations, veterans groups and local service clubs often book their outing at the town course. These events tend to be friendly, affordable and easy to get into. This guide covers why munis are such common outing hosts, what to expect and how to find one.

## What makes a course municipal

A municipal course is owned by a local government. Some are run directly by a parks department; others are managed by a private company under contract. Either way, they're open to the public, and many offer resident rates. Courses range from short nine-hole layouts to full 18-hole championship courses that have hosted state and regional tournaments.

On Golf Outing Finder, municipal courses are grouped with public courses under the "municipal and public" course type. That's because, from a player's point of view, the experience is similar: anyone can book, and outing fees tend to be on the lower end.

## Why munis host so many outings

**They're local.** The organizers of a youth hockey fundraiser or a high school booster outing often live near the town course, and so do most of their players.

**They're affordable for organizers.** A lower per-player cost from the course means more of each entry fee can go to the cause, or the organizer can keep the entry price low.

**They're used to it.** Many municipal courses host outings every week in season and have a routine for shotgun starts, cart staging, scoring and food.

**They're accessible.** Players of all skill levels feel at home. A muni outing is often where people play their first scramble.

## What they cost

Municipal outings are often the most affordable outings you'll find. As an illustrative range, a weekday muni scramble might be $100 to $175 per player, and a weekend or larger event might be $150 to $250. Foursome prices are often listed too, sometimes with a small discount. Prices vary by region, course and what's included.

A low price doesn't mean a lesser event. A $125 muni scramble can be a well-run, full-field outing with a good lunch, a lively dinner and real money raised for a cause. For a closer look at the numbers, see [how outing pricing works](/guides/how-outing-pricing-works).

## What's usually included

A typical municipal outing fee covers:

- 18 holes of golf
- a shared cart
- range balls, at courses that have a range
- lunch, often a grill line or boxed lunch
- dinner or a cookout after the round, at many outings
- contests like closest to the pin and longest drive

Some include a small gift, like a sleeve of balls or a towel. Drinks on the course might be included through drink tickets or sold from the beverage cart. Each listing on our site shows what's included, and our guide on [what an entry fee includes](/guides/what-an-entry-fee-includes) covers the details.

## When munis host outings

Municipal courses are busiest with regular play on weekends, so many outings happen on weekdays, especially Mondays and Fridays. Some munis close the course for a morning or afternoon shotgun, and others run a shotgun for the outing and open the course to the public afterward.

Season depends on where you live. In the northern half of the country, most muni outings run from May through September, with a burst in early fall. In the South and Southwest, outings run much of the year, with the hottest summer months often slower and spring and fall the busiest.

## What to expect on the day

**Arrive early.** Plan on 60 to 90 minutes before the shotgun. Check in at the registration table, often set up near the clubhouse or under a tent.

**Dress.** Most munis are relaxed about dress, but a collared shirt or golf top is the safe choice for an outing. Some allow denim; some don't. Check the course's site if you're unsure.

**The pace.** Muni outings often run a full field of 120 to 144 players. Keep moving, and pick up your ball once your team has chosen a shot.

**The crowd.** Expect a mix of players, from people who golf every week to people who play once a year at this outing. Most groups are there to have fun and support a cause.

**The clubhouse.** Some munis have a full restaurant; others have a snack bar and a patio. Dinner might be in a banquet room or under a tent outside.

## How to find muni outings

1. **Filter by course type.** On Golf Outing Finder, choose "municipal and public" to show outings at those courses.
2. **Set a distance.** Choose 10, 25, 50 or 100 miles from where you are.
3. **Set a price cap.** Use the maximum price per player filter to stay within budget.
4. **Pick a format.** Most muni outings are scrambles, but some are best ball or shamble.
5. **Look for singles welcome.** If you don't have a full team, this filter shows outings that list a single price.

Check the "Last verified" date on each listing, and use the Register button to sign up on the organizer's page. We don't sell entries; registration and payment happen with the organizer.

## Supporting your local course

Outings matter to many municipal courses. The revenue helps pay for maintenance, staff and improvements, and the events bring new players to the course. If you enjoy the day, consider coming back for a regular round.
